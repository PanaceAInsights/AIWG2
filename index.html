Incremental Risk Model
NSW operational coordination

1Source data capture (Vectra WB360)

3D total body photography captured at each site. Output formats: CR2 (raw camera files, identifiable), JSON (de-identified lesion metadata), TOM (3D mesh files). Stored in site-local Vectra clinical database.

2Cleansed export from Vectra DB

VectraDBTool + exporter runs locally at each site. Removes patient name, DOB, ID. Retains External ID for REDCap linkage. Output: per-patient .db file plus CR2, JSON, TOM folders.

runs site-local

3aNaevus burden processing (Script 1)

Reads JSON files. Filters by confidence (≥80%) and major axis (≥2 mm). Outputs CSV of naevi per body site per participant. Broad location labels (e.g. "Left Leg" without upper/lower) affect ~7% of participants.

processing time ~minutes

3bPhotodamage processing (Script 2)

CR2 → JPEG conversion → tile into 9×5 grid → HSV/YCbCr skin filter → AI model inference per tile. Aggregation methodology to per-body-site and per-individual under discussion.

heavier processing · hours

3cFreckling processing

7 body-location CR2 views → PNG conversion → region-specific extraction (arms rotated 90°, back ROI until skin >75%, face cheek crop via MediaPipe). Algorithm currently being externally validated.

~20h export per cohort

4Analysis-ready outputs → downstream projects

Phenotype-specific CSVs feed into the eight downstream projects. Most analyses currently waiting on dataset completion across sites and pathology review of outcomes.

Enablers and blockers

Forces currently supporting progress, and items awaiting resolution.

Enablers supporting progress

  • ISIC tile extraction tool now functional. Filter paper progressing on Alfred data.
  • Updated Canfield CR2 conversion utility resolves earlier image format incompatibility.
  • Script 1 distributed across sites, with first extraction results from PA presented.
  • Existing ethics framework covers the Dexi 3.0 evaluation without requiring new submission.
  • Two risk-model workstreams formalised as distinct projects (deep learning vs interpretable incremental).
  • MIA access established for NSW work, enabling baseline risk data extraction and local script execution.
  • Operations coordination role centralised with Sam Polkinghorne-Katz across workstreams.

Blockers awaiting resolution

  • Photodamage AI model distribution. Model being prepared for sharing with the wider extraction team for Script 2 integration.
  • Photodamage aggregation methodology. Per-tile outputs need translation to per-body-site and per-individual scores for analysis.
  • Pathology review of ~100 outstanding melanoma slides is a prerequisite for outcome-based analyses across multiple projects.
  • NSW cross-site data sharing pathway unresolved. No national repository yet. Working assumption: distributed extraction.
  • Freckling external validation awaiting Alfred image transfer to UQ and annotator capacity.
  • Filter paper labelling approach. Distinction between "lesions for dermoscopy" vs "lesions for biopsy" being defined.
  • Per-site sequential extraction. Image data must be cleaned at each site before scripts can run.

Speculative · Experimental · Conversation Starters

What this infrastructure could become

The ACEMID cohort, the imaging pipeline, and the AIWG capabilities together create a research substrate that could support much more than the current eight projects. Some directions worth considering — none are commitments, but each is grounded in capabilities we already have or are close to having.

01 · METHODS Methodological extensions Where the AI work could go beyond cross-sectional prediction
001

Temporal phenotypic trajectory

Most participants will have multiple Vectra captures during follow-up. Risk prediction from rate of phenotypic change (mole growth velocity, freckling accumulation, photodamage progression) rather than baseline state alone. Untested in melanoma literature; ACEMID is uniquely positioned to do it first.

NEEDSLongitudinal data, ≥2 visits per participant STATUSFeasible from 2027
002

Federated multimodal model

Train a unified risk model across QLD, VIC, and NSW without raw imaging data ever leaving each site. Structurally solves the cross-state data sharing constraints we keep working around. Methods publication in its own right.

NEEDSFederated learning framework, site IT alignment STATUSProof-of-concept ready
003

Visual SHAP on 3D avatars

Take feature-level interpretability further. Attention maps overlaid directly on the patient's 3D avatar showing where on their body the model is drawing risk signal from. Research artifact → clinical UI in a single step.

NEEDSSHAP outputs from incremental risk model STATUSAchievable once P06 has results
004

"Skin age" composite biomarker

A continuous index combining UV damage, freckling, mole burden, and skin colour evolution — analogous to biological age in epigenetics. Could become a single interpretable number for screening triage and patient communication.

NEEDSAll four image markers + age data STATUSConcept stage
02 · CLINICAL Clinical translation pathways From research output to deployed practice
005

AI-augmented dermatologist study

Prospective head-to-head: dermatologist alone vs dermatologist + AI second reader. Could become the first definitive study of TBP-AI augmentation in routine surveillance. Natural extension of the Automate Me trial infrastructure.

NEEDSTrial design, validated AI model STATUSDiscussions could start now
006

Lightweight GP-deployable tool

A pared-down risk model using features extractable from a smartphone or basic clinical camera — no Vectra required. Massive scalability story for primary care, rural, and global health contexts where TBP isn't feasible.

NEEDSFeature ablation analysis, validation cohort STATUSFuture direction
007

MIA clinical UI integration

Embed the validated incremental risk model directly into MIA's clinical workflow as a decision support tool. From research output to bedside in a single institution. Bridges the methods-to-impact gap that most academic AI work never crosses.

NEEDSMIA partnership, validated P06 outputs STATUSPipeline forming
03 · CROSS-APPLICATION Beyond melanoma Same infrastructure, different outcomes
008

Keratinocyte cancer prediction

ACEMID participants develop KCs at higher rates than melanoma. The cohort and imaging are already collected; only additional outcome linkage is needed. Two cancer endpoints from a single infrastructure substantially increases the cost-effectiveness story.

NEEDSOutcome linkage for BCC and SCC STATUSSubstudy proposal
009

Pre-treatment immunotoxicity signature

Building on the CuTox work — use ACEMID-style TBP imaging on patients before they start immunotherapy to predict who develops immune skin toxicity. Novel patient population, same imaging methodology.

NEEDSMIA immunotherapy cohort, baseline TBP STATUSLinked to Linda's CuTox work
010

Pigmented lesion → uveal melanoma connection

Phenotypic markers of cutaneous melanoma risk may share biology with uveal melanoma susceptibility. Exploratory work linking cutaneous phenotype to ocular outcomes via registry data could open an underexplored research area.

NEEDSUveal registry linkage, ophthalmology partnership STATUSHypothesis stage
04 · POLICY & EQUITY Population-level impact Where the methods meet the screening program
011

ACEMID as international validation hub

Position the cohort as the global gold-standard external validation resource for new melanoma AI. Dexi 3.0 is the first; every subsequent commercial or academic tool could be benchmarked here. Strategic infrastructure positioning.

NEEDSGovernance framework, evaluation protocol STATUSDexi 3.0 sets precedent
012

Equity-stratified performance audit

Dedicated subgroup analyses across Fitzpatrick type, age, sex, and ancestry on every AI tool the group develops. Pre-emptively answers regulatory and reviewer questions. Aligns with REP-AI ethics work.

NEEDSDemographic data linkage, subgroup sample sizes STATUSCan run alongside P05 and P06
013

Roadmap cost-effectiveness modelling

Feed the enhanced risk model outputs into Markov state-transition models comparing screening strategies. Direct translation to Cancer Australia's National Skin Cancer Screening Roadmap. Connects AIWG methods work to national policy.

NEEDSValidated risk model, health economics partnership STATUSDependent on P06 completion
This page is a thinking space, not a roadmap. Ideas here are deliberately exploratory — some may already be in development at one of the sites, others may have been considered and dismissed for reasons not captured here. Suggestions and additions welcome.
